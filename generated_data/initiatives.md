# Brightwater Packaging Systems, Inc.
## Key Initiatives and Roadmap

### Context & Framing
**Planning horizon:** 18 months, April 1, 2025 through September 30, 2026

**Strategic goals**
- Improve on-time delivery and schedule predictability across custom machine and retrofit programs.
- Increase capacity flexibility and cost competitiveness by ramping the Queretaro plant without compromising quality.
- Reduce supply-chain concentration risk and exposure to part obsolescence in motion and controls components.
- Improve execution discipline across engineering, planning, purchasing, manufacturing, and field deployment.
- Grow higher-margin recurring aftermarket revenue through parts, service contracts, and remote support.
- Standardize repeatable retrofit offerings for installed-base customers to improve win rate, lead time, and margin.

**Driving principles**
- Protect customer commitments first, especially for active builds and startup schedules.
- Standardize where possible while preserving Brightwater’s custom-engineering value proposition.
- Treat supplier performance, engineering changes, and customer specification changes as managed execution risks, not exceptions.
- Build processes that support both Dayton and Queretaro as a coordinated operating footprint.
- Maintain clean system data and reporting discipline through the ERP transition.

## 1) Queretaro Plant Ramp
**Owner:** VP Operations  
**Priority:** P0

**Description and scope**
Shift a defined share of case-packer and retrofit-kit build activity to Queretaro; hire and train assembly and controls staff; qualify local machining, sheet-metal, and purchased-component suppliers; establish the plant as a repeatable build center for selected product families. Target 30% of total build hours in Queretaro by the end of the planning window.

**Current status**
Partially complete. Hiring and pilot transfers were completed, but supplier qualification and stable transfer of full-rate build hours trailed plan. As of late September 2026, Queretaro is carrying meaningful case-packer and retrofit-kit work but remains below the build-hour target.

**Key milestones with target dates**
- 2025-04-15: Transfer scope approved for initial case-packer and retrofit-kit families.
- 2025-05-31: Plant staffing plan approved; recruiting opened for assemblers, test technicians, and controls support.
- 2025-07-15: First wave of assembly hires completed.
- 2025-08-30: Cross-site training completed for first 12 Queretaro assembly employees.
- 2025-09-20: Pilot retrofit-kit build completed in Queretaro.
- 2025-11-15: First case-packer subassembly build released to Queretaro.
- 2026-01-31: Three local machining suppliers and two sheet-metal suppliers qualified for production use.
- 2026-03-31: Queretaro reaches 18% of monthly build hours.
- 2026-06-30: Local controls panel assembly capability established for selected programs.
- 2026-08-31: Re-planned target of 26% monthly build hours in Queretaro.
- 2026-09-30: 30% total build-hour target for the planning window.

**Dependencies and risks**
- Late supplier deliveries in Mexico affecting pilot and repeat builds.
- Quality escapes during transfer of case-packer and retrofit-kit work.
- Tribal knowledge transfer gaps between Dayton and Queretaro teams.
- Mid-build ECOs and customer specification changes disrupting build standardization.
- Part obsolescence in PLC/HMI components complicating common build packages across sites.
- Freight disputes and damage on cross-border material movement and shipped retrofit kits.

**Success criteria / KPIs**
- Queretaro reaches 30% of total build hours by 2026-09-30.
- First-pass FAT pass rate on Queretaro-led builds at or above 92%.
- Rework hours on transferred product families reduced to less than 6% of build hours by end of window.
- At least five local suppliers approved and carrying active production releases.
- Cross-site training completed for 100% of targeted Queretaro assembly and controls staff.

## 2) Dual-Sourcing of Servo Drives and Motors
**Owner:** Purchasing with Controls Engineering  
**Priority:** P0

**Description and scope**
Qualify a second vendor for servo drives and motors to reduce reliance on the incumbent vendor that caused line-down events and late-ship incidents. Update controls architecture, panel layouts, software standards, and purchasing rules to support both vendors. Re-quote affected open jobs and manage customer approvals for substitutions where required.

**Current status**
Substantially advanced but not fully closed. Second-source technical qualification was completed on core machine families, but customer approvals and open-job re-quoting ran later than plan. Remaining exposure is concentrated in jobs carrying the legacy drive family and in builds impacted by the PLC/HMI end-of-life cycle.

**Key milestones with target dates**
- 2025-04-25: Second-source vendor shortlist approved.
- 2025-06-10: Lab testing started for alternate servo drives and motors.
- 2025-08-05: Controls compatibility matrix completed for both vendors.
- 2025-09-30: Initial dual-vendor panel design standards released.
- 2025-11-14: Qualification build completed on first machine using alternate drive family.
- 2026-01-20: Customer-facing substitution language and approval workflow issued.
- 2026-02-28: Open-job exposure review completed for all jobs with legacy drives on order or quoted.
- 2026-04-15: Re-quote package completed for affected open jobs.
- 2026-06-30: 60% of eligible new releases converted to dual-source standard.
- 2026-09-30: 85% of eligible new releases converted; remaining legacy exceptions documented.

**Dependencies and risks**
- Firmware and tuning differences between vendors affecting machine performance and startup.
- Customer approval delays for substitutions on active or already-approved jobs.
- Obsolescence of the old drive family creating forced redesigns mid-build.
- PLC/HMI end-of-life requiring simultaneous controls changes beyond motion hardware.
- Late supplier deliveries on both primary and alternate motion components.
- ECOs created by panel redesigns, field feedback, or revised customer standards.

**Success criteria / KPIs**
- 85% of eligible new releases support both approved drive/motor vendors by 2026-09-30.
- Single-vendor line-down incidents reduced to zero on active builds after 2026-06-30.
- Average quote-cycle delay from motion-component shortages reduced by 50% versus Q2 2025 baseline.
- 100% of active jobs exposed to the legacy drive family reviewed and dispositioned.
- Startup issues attributable to alternate drive firmware held below 3 issues per 10 machine startups.

## 3) ERP Migration from the Legacy System to a New ERP
**Owner:** Finance + IT + Planning  
**Priority:** P0

**Description and scope**
Migrate from the legacy ERP to a new platform covering customer, vendor, item, purchasing, planning, inventory, production, and financial workflows. New ERP records will assign new vendor and customer IDs; legacy IDs will remain visible in emails, quotes, and historical purchase orders during the transition period. Part numbers will migrate through a maintained mapping table and cross-reference structure.

**Current status**
Go-live occurred mid-window, followed by extended stabilization. Core transactions are running in the new ERP, but record cleanup, promised-date accuracy on carried-over open POs, and reporting consistency remained open through the end of the window.

**Key milestones with target dates**
- 2025-04-30: ERP implementation charter, governance, and workstream owners approved.
- 2025-06-15: Customer, vendor, and part master-data cleanup started.
- 2025-08-01: Part-number mapping table frozen for mock conversion cycle 1.
- 2025-09-12: Mock conversion cycle 1 completed.
- 2025-10-31: Open PO carryover rules and promised-date conversion logic approved.
- 2025-12-05: User acceptance testing completed for purchasing, planning, inventory, and finance workflows.
- 2026-01-04: New ERP go-live.
- 2026-01-05 to 2026-02-28: Hypercare period for order entry, purchasing, receiving, inventory, and financial close.
- 2026-03-31: Legacy-to-new ID cross-reference tools deployed for customer service, purchasing, and finance.
- 2026-05-15: Duplicate vendor and customer record cleanup wave 1 completed.
- 2026-07-31: Open PO promised-date audit and corrections completed.
- 2026-09-30: Reporting stabilization and master-data governance handoff completed.

**Dependencies and risks**
- Duplicate vendor records introduced during migration and ongoing use of legacy identifiers.
- Open POs carried over with incorrect promised dates affecting planning and supplier follow-up.
- Reporting gaps during cutover impacting backlog, on-time delivery, inventory, and margin visibility.
- Ongoing use of old customer and vendor IDs in email, quotes, and legacy POs creating reconciliation delays.
- Part-number mapping errors affecting procurement, inventory transactions, and build execution.
- ECOs, part obsolescence, and supplier substitutions increasing item-master complexity during conversion.

**Success criteria / KPIs**
- 98% master-data accuracy for active customers, vendors, and items by 2026-06-30.
- 100% of active part numbers mapped to new ERP IDs with searchable legacy cross-reference.
- Less than 2% of open POs carrying incorrect promised dates after the 2026-07-31 audit.
- Month-end close returned to normal timing by the second close after go-live.
- Core operating reports for backlog, bookings, OTD, inventory, and gross margin available without manual reconciliation by 2026-09-30.

## 4) Aftermarket Push
**Owner:** VP Aftermarket / Field Service  
**Priority:** P1

**Description and scope**
Increase combined spare parts and service contract revenue from 30% to 35% of total revenue through an installed-base growth program. Focus areas include targeted installed-base campaigns, model-based parts kits, formal service-contract renewal management, and a remote-support offering.

**Current status**
In progress with measurable gains in parts-kit adoption and renewal discipline. Remote support launched later than originally planned, and full revenue-mix impact remains short of target entering the final days of the window.

**Key milestones with target dates**
- 2025-05-15: Installed-base segmentation completed by machine family, age, and service history.
- 2025-07-01: Top 50 aftermarket accounts assigned to account plans.
- 2025-08-22: Standard parts-kit structure approved for five highest-volume machine models.
- 2025-10-15: Service-contract renewal calendar and ownership process launched.
- 2025-12-01: First targeted installed-base campaign launched for preventive maintenance and wear-parts kits.
- 2026-02-15: Remote-support commercial offering defined and priced.
- 2026-04-01: Remote-support pilot live with 10 customer sites.
- 2026-06-30: Parts kits available for 12 machine models.
- 2026-08-31: Renewal process expanded to 100% of expiring service contracts.
- 2026-09-30: Aftermarket revenue mix target review.

**Dependencies and risks**
- ERP customer and part-ID changes complicating installed-base data, renewals, quoting, and order history.
- Late supplier deliveries affecting spare-parts fill rate and service-kit availability.
- Freight damage and freight claim disputes on urgent parts shipments and retrofit-related spares.
- Part obsolescence in PLC/HMI families requiring replacement-kit engineering instead of like-for-like spares.
- Field-service capacity constraints limiting contract attach and remote-support adoption.

**Success criteria / KPIs**
- Combined spare parts and service revenue reaches 35% of total revenue by 2026-09-30.
- Service-contract renewal rate reaches 85% on expiring contracts.
- Parts-kit attachment rate reaches 25% on eligible installed-base opportunities.
- Remote-support offering reaches 25 contracted customer sites by end of window.
- Parts fill rate improves to 95% for stocked kit components.

## 5) Beverage-Customer Retrofit Program
**Owner:** Applications Engineering + Sales  
**Priority:** P1

**Description and scope**
Develop and commercialize a standard retrofit package for beverage customers converting capping and case-packing lines to new container formats. Run several customer programs in parallel while standardizing application assumptions, estimating, kit content, controls scope, field-install approach, and freight packaging.

**Current status**
Commercial traction is strong, with multiple customer programs launched in parallel. Execution variability remains elevated due to customer specification changes after PO, mid-build ECOs, and freight damage on retrofit kits.

**Key milestones with target dates**
- 2025-04-18: Beverage retrofit program charter approved.
- 2025-06-05: Standard scope, assumptions, and quotation template released.
- 2025-07-25: First two pilot customer retrofit proposals issued.
- 2025-09-10: First pilot order received.
- 2025-11-30: Standard mechanical and controls package released for capping retrofit baseline.
- 2026-01-22: Standard case-packing retrofit kit released.
- 2026-03-15: Three customer programs active in parallel.
- 2026-05-31: Standard freight packaging and shipment checklist implemented.
- 2026-07-31: Five customer programs active in parallel.
- 2026-09-30: Post-install performance review completed for first wave of customer sites.

**Dependencies and risks**
- Customer specification changes after PO increasing scope, lead time, and commercial exposure.
- ECOs during active build due to format details, legacy line conditions, or controls-interface changes.
- Freight damage on retrofit kits delaying installation windows and startup.
- Late supplier deliveries on machined change parts, sheet metal, drives, and controls components.
- PLC/HMI end-of-life requiring alternate components in standard retrofit packages.
- Field-service scheduling conflicts across overlapping customer shutdown windows.

**Success criteria / KPIs**
- At least six beverage retrofit orders booked during the planning window.
- Average engineering hours per retrofit reduced 20% from first pilot to fifth program.
- Gross margin on standard retrofit packages at or above target on 75% of completed jobs.
- On-site installation completed within scheduled shutdown window on 90% of completed projects.
- Freight-damage incidents on retrofit kits reduced below 2% of shipments after packaging standard rollout.

## 6) Supplier Quality Program
**Owner:** Quality with Purchasing  
**Priority:** P1

**Description and scope**
Formalize supplier quality management for machining and sheet-metal suppliers using NCRs, supplier corrective action requests, scorecards, and on-time-delivery tracking. Tighten performance review cadence and remove the worst-performing suppliers that do not improve.

**Current status**
Program infrastructure is in place and scorecarding is active. Corrective actions improved visibility and accountability, but sustained supplier performance gains remain uneven. Final supplier disqualifications are in process at the end of the window.

**Key milestones with target dates**
- 2025-05-10: Supplier quality program charter approved.
- 2025-06-20: NCR and SCAR workflow standardized across Quality and Purchasing.
- 2025-08-15: Top 20 machining and sheet-metal suppliers enrolled in monthly scorecards.
- 2025-10-01: On-time-delivery and defect metrics added to supplier review cadence.
- 2026-01-31: First formal quarterly supplier business review cycle completed.
- 2026-04-30: Corrective-action closure audit completed for bottom-quartile suppliers.
- 2026-06-15: Supplier rationalization recommendation submitted.
- 2026-08-15: Two worst-performing suppliers disqualified and release plan shifted.
- 2026-09-30: Replacement suppliers fully loaded into sourcing plan.

**Dependencies and risks**
- Late supplier deliveries remain a chronic issue and may initially worsen as poor suppliers are exited.
- Capacity constraints at replacement suppliers during transition.
- ERP promised-date and vendor-record issues affecting performance measurement accuracy.
- ECOs and customer specification changes creating demand volatility that complicates supplier scorecard interpretation.
- Freight claim disputes obscuring root cause between supplier packaging and carrier handling.
- Part obsolescence causing urgent buys outside preferred supplier channels.

**Success criteria / KPIs**
- Supplier OTD for top 20 machining and sheet-metal suppliers improved to 90% by end of window.
- Supplier defect rate reduced 30% versus Q2 2025 baseline.
- 100% of major supplier NCRs assigned with owner and due date within five business days.
- 80% of SCARs closed on time.
- Two worst-performing suppliers disqualified and replaced without material increase in missed ship dates.

## Success Criteria

### Initiative-level success metrics
- **Queretaro plant ramp:** 30% of total build hours in Queretaro; first-pass FAT pass rate at or above 92%; transferred-family rework below 6%.
- **Dual-sourcing of servo drives and motors:** 85% of eligible new releases on dual-source standard; zero single-vendor line-down incidents after 2026-06-30; all legacy-drive exposure dispositioned.
- **ERP migration:** 98% master-data accuracy; less than 2% open-PO promised-date errors after audit; core operating reports stable without manual reconciliation.
- **Aftermarket push:** combined parts and service revenue at 35% of total revenue; 85% service-contract renewal rate; remote support at 25 contracted sites.
- **Beverage-customer retrofit program:** at least six retrofit orders booked; 20% reduction in engineering hours from pilot to repeat jobs; 90% of installs completed within shutdown windows.
- **Supplier quality program:** 90% supplier OTD for top 20 suppliers; 30% defect-rate reduction; two poor-performing suppliers exited.

### Overall roadmap evaluation criteria
- Company on-time delivery performance improves despite ERP cutover, supplier transitions, and component obsolescence.
- Backlog execution improves through lower schedule slippage from late suppliers, ECOs, and post-PO customer specification changes.
- Gross-margin performance improves on repeatable product families, retrofit programs, and aftermarket offerings.
- Aftermarket mix increases while protecting service responsiveness and installed-base customer satisfaction.
- Cross-site operating model between Dayton and Queretaro becomes more repeatable with fewer quality escapes and lower transfer friction.
- Supply-chain concentration risk is reduced in motion controls, machining, and sheet-metal categories.
- Management reporting is reliable enough to support planning, purchasing, operations, and monthly performance review without parallel spreadsheets.
